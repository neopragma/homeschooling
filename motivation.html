Main page => Motivation

Motivated learners will benefit more from your teaching and guidance than unmotivated learners, and they will enjoy the homeschooling experience more, too. Motivated learners will be easier to manage and guide through the curriculum, and to keep on track with your state's educational standards.

Keeping your learners motivated will require mindful effort on your part; there are no "standard" answers. You need information in three key areas, as well as patience and a willingness to adapt based on how your learners respond to your motivational efforts.

The three key areas to learn about are:

  1. How human motivation works
  2. Understanding your learners
  3. Techniques that others have found effective

How human motivation works

Our understanding of human motivation is constantly changing as researchers and practitioners advance their knowledge. Currently, the leading theory of motivation is called Self-Determination Theory, or SDT. Here is a summary of the six levels of motivation (or self-determination) described in SDT, as documented in the article Motivation & Self-Determination Theory by Mike Bowler.

Amotivated. No desire to do it. If it happens at all then it was an accident or a side effect of something else.

External. "I was told to do it" and there are likely external punishments and/or rewards involved.
Ed. - This is where positive and negative reinforcement get you. There will be times when this is the only way to get your learners moving, but if you depend on this all the time you will quickly become exhausted.

Introjected. Internalized nagging. "I feel that I should do it to meet my own goals." This is driven by self-control and internal rewards or punishments.
Ed. - This may apply to older learners, but is unlikely to come into play with younger ones.

Identified. "Doing this will help me achieve my goals." I may not care about this activity for itself but doing it will move me closer to a goal that I do care about.

Integrated. Doing this is part of who I am. "I volunteer at a food bank because I am kind." Behavior tied to our identity.

Intrinsic. "I love this - it feels great." Enjoyable for its own sake.

SDT treats External, Introjected, Identified, and Integrated as forms of extrinsic motivation. Ideally, we would like learners to be intrinsically motivated for all academic and skill-oriented activities, but as a practical matter Identified and Integrated motivation are powerful as well.

Understanding your learners

Individuals are motivated or demotivated by different factors, including (but not limited to)

  • Age or developmental stage
  • Sex
  • Neurodiversity
  • Emotional issues
  • Learner's relationship with you

When you are working with more than one learner, the picture quickly becomes complex. Additional factors can affect the dynamics of the learning environment in ways that motivate or demotivate each individual, such as

  • Age differences - learners at different academic levels and developmental stages
  • Sex - two boys interact differently than two girls, and differently than one of each
  • Competition for your attention/approval
  • Behavioral issues whose causes you don't know, when some of your learners are not your own children
  • Neurodiversity - how learners interact with each other vs. when they are alone

Professional teachers receive training in classroom management as well as some basic education in psychology. Even then, many struggle with these issues. As a homeschooler who may not have been trained for this, you must be prepared for some friction and be ready to practice patience.
