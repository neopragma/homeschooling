Board Games

This page lists board games that offer educational value.

Games are listed in alphabetical order by name. They are not broken out by age group, genre, learning goals, or subject matter.

Antiquity Quest (Grandpa Beck's Games)

Ages: 10 and up

Fantasy adventure game in which players try to assemble the most prestigious collection of antiquities and treasures while undermining other players' collections by adding incompatible items into them. Helps with learning

  • Strategy
  • Patience
  • Long-term planning
  • Sportsmanship

Battleship (Hasbro)

Ages: 6 and up

Players try to sink their opponent's fleet by choosing coordinates on a battle grid. Suitable for young children.

  • Memory skills
  • Strategy
  • Sportsmanship

Betrayal at House on the Hill (Green)

Ages: 12 and up

Cooperative mystery game. Players build the haunted mansion piece by piece as the game progresses. The players must cooperate to win, but one of them is secretly a traitor.

  • Cooperation
  • Problem-solving
  • Long-term planning
  • Sportsmanship

Blokus (Blokus)

Ages: 6 and up

Players fit Tetris-like shapes onto a grid according to a few simple rules. For adults, it is a time-waster for a rainy day. For children, it can help with the following:

  • Strategy
  • Patience
  • Spatial relationships
  • Sportsmanship

Camp (Education Outdoors)

Ages: 6 and up

Not highly competitive, Camp is designed for education about nature and the outdoors. There are multiple levels of difficulty so that players of different ages can play together.

  • Science
  • Geography
  • Animals
  • Critical thinking

Castle Panic (Fireside Games)

Ages: 6 and up (depending on version)

Described as a "cooperative castle defense game," players must collaborate to defend the castle against monsters that come out of the surrounding forest. Flexible rules allow for customizing the level of difficulty. Several different versions are available. There is a "junior" version called My First Castle Panic, aimed at younger children.

  • Strategy
  • Long-term thinking
  • Cooperation

Chameleon (Big Potato)

Ages: 14 and up

One player is secretly the Chameleon. The other players must determine who the Chameleon is. There are hidden codes and secret words. Game play is fast.

  • Deduction
  • Memory skills

Covalence (Genius Games)

Ages: 10 and up

A cooperative game focusing on basic chemistry concepts including covalent bonding, elements, chemical nomenclature, stereochemistry, and organic chemistry.

  • Chemistry
  • Problem-solving
  • Cooperation

Diplomacy (Hasbro)

Ages: 12 and up

A cooperative game based on European diplomacy set at the turn of the 20th century, before World War I.

  • History
  • Strategy
  • Cooperation
  • Sportsmanship

Math Rush (Genius Games)

Ages: 6 and up (depending on version)

There are three volumes: Volume 1 covers addition and subtraction, Volume 2 covers fractions and decimals, and Volume 3 covers multiplication and exponents. Designed to build rapid computational skills for elementary arithmetic and to build memory skills.

  • Mental math
  • Cooperation

Monopoly (Hasbro)

Ages: 4 and up (depending on version)

Many versions of this classic game are available, featuring different locales or themes. There are also a number of Monopoly Junior versions, geared for younger children. Monopoly can help teach the following skills, at least:

  • Math
  • Money management
  • Memory skills
  • Strategy
  • Patience
  • Sportsmanship

Mysterium (Libellud)

Ages: 10 and up

One player plays the victim of a murder, now a ghost. The other players play psychics who must solve the mystery of the murder by communicating with the victim. Learning opportunities:

  • Cooperation
  • Memory skills
  • Problem-solving
  • Patience

Problem Solved! (Problem Solved!)

Ages: 5 and up

A fast-paced arithmetic game suitable for young learners.

  • Math
  • Speed math
  • Math memorization
  • Team play

Robot Turtles (ThinkFun)

Ages: 4 to 11

Board game version of elementary computer programming activities based on Logo. The game is designed for families to play together (parents and children). Kids write "computer programs" with playing cards (not with an actual computer). It helps teach the following skills:

  • Logic
  • Programming principles
  • Collaboration

Trekking the National Parks (Underdog Games)

Ages: 6 and up

The game was developed by a family after they had visited all the national parks in the United States. The game is highly rated by homeschoolers, but be aware it is significantly more expensive than most board games.

  • Strategy
  • Geography
  • Cooperation
  • Memory skills
  • Patience
  • Sportsmanship

Trivial Pursuit (Hasbro)

Ages: 6 and up

Trivia game. Many categories of questions. Multiple editions and expansion packs available.

  • General knowledge

What Next?

Ages: 8 and up

Action adventure board game designed for families (adults and kids playing together). Players choose how to deal with various challenges collaboratively. There are over 60 specific challenges or mini-games. Warning: Relatively expensive compared to most board games.

  • Strategy
  • Cooperation
  • Sportsmanship

Wit's End

Ages: 16 and up

Trivia game.

  • Logic
  • Problem-solving
