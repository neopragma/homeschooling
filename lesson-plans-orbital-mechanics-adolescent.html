Sample Lesson Plan for "Orbital Mechanics", Adolescent Level

Item Content
Grade Level(s) 7-9
Learning Objectives Understand the basics of orbital mechanics.
Prerequisites Math up to the pre-algebra level. Awareness of basic geometry. There is no heavy math in this lesson.
Duration 45 minutes not counting Kerbal Space Program game play time.
00:10. Watch the video Watch the video, "Orbits and Kepler's Laws."
00:25. Guided discussion Points to bring out:
  • Planets orbit the Sun counterclockwise when solar system viewed from above the sun's north pole.
  • Planetary orbits are aligned with the Ecliptic Plane.
  • First property of an ellipse - there are two foci. Sum of distances from any point on the ellipse to the foci is always a constant
  • Second property of an ellipse - eccentricity (amount of flattening) is between 0 (circle) and 1 (parabola)
  • Third property of an ellipse - longest axis is the major axis, shortest axis is the minor axis; half the major axis is a semi-major axis
  • Kepler's 1st Law: Each planet's orbit around the sun is an ellipse. The sun's center is always located at one focus of the orbital ellipse.
  • Kepler's 2nd Law: Law of Areas. The imaginary line joining a planet and the sun sweeps equal areas of space in equal time intervals as the planet orbits.
  • Kepler's 3rd Law: Law of Periods. The squares of the orbital periods of the planets are directly proportional to the cubes of the semi-major axes of their orbits. This means planets closer to the sun orbit faster than planets farther from the sun.
  • Perehelion: The point of closest approach of a planet to the sun. Planet moves fastest.
  • Aphelion: The point of greatest separation between a planet to the sun. Planet moves slowest.
  • -helion comes from the Greek word, helios, the sun. The Greek word, ge means Earth. So in an orbit around the Earth, we say perigee and apogee. This is where we get the prefix geo-, as in geometry, geocentric, geosynchronous, and other words.
00:10. Assessment Distribute copies of the "Orbital Mechanics Adolescent Assessment" document. Ask learners to complete it. Then discuss the results.
01:00. Play Kerbal Space Program Let learners play the Kerbal Space Program game.
Preparation
  • Physical or electronic rewards that you want to give the learners for completing the lesson.
  • Download and make copies of the "Kepler's Laws Transcript" that accompanies the video.
  • Make copies of the Orbital Mechanics Adolescent Assessment document.
  • Cue up video:
    • Orbits and Kepler's Laws (NASA Science, Solar System Exploration) - video and transcript
  • Prepare the game, "Kerbal Space Program," to the point that orbital mechanics come into play. Game home page: https://www.kerbalspaceprogram.com/.
