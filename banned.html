Banned!

Dave Nicolette

When the government wants to control how your children think so they will grow up to be obedient servants of the State, it's time to homeschool.

There's nothing particularly new about books being banned in schools. Parents, teachers, religious orders, and government officials have disapproved of certain books ever since public schooling was first instituted in the US. Many titles are legitimately not suitable for children; but when the list of banned books clearly follows a pattern that reflects a political movement, the Republic is in danger.

As this is written, there is a strong and growing movement across the United states to ban books and other materials in public schools. There has always been a banned book list for public schools, and at times the selections have seemed almost comically harmless. But today, the list of banned materials is very long, and includes such non-political subjects as mathematics.

Texas, 2021-2022

In Texas, educators are being asked by the government to look actively for educational material to ban, and to report it to the State - that is, to denounce colleagues and parents, under laws specifically designed to protect the authorities from any public scrutiny or oversight.

The move is being justified on the basis of Texas law, section 301.020(a)(4) - "Each general investigating committee may initiate or continue inquiries and hearings concerning...any other matter the committee considers necessary for the information of the legislature or for the welfare and protection of state citizens." Furthermore, section 301.020(e) states, "Information held by a general investigating committee is confidential and not subject to public disclosure except as provided by the rules of the house establishing the committee."

The law provides for secret investigations into literally anything, with no accountability, no public oversight, and no mechanism to question or dispute the findings of the investigations.

Texas State Representative Matt Krause, Republican from Ft. Worth, sent a letter to school districts on October 25, 2021, asking them to review a 16-page list of books that he felt contained "objectionable content." See the online article at boingboing.net/2021/10/30/texas-lawmakers-considering-a-ban-on-850-books-heres-the-full-list.html. In case that page is taken down, we have saved a copy of the list here.

Krause seeks to ban "material that might make students feel discomfort, guilt, anguish, or any other form of psychological distress because of their race or sex or convey that a student, by virtue of their race or sex, is inherently racist, sexist, or oppressive, whether consciously or unconsciously." Clearly, this is a reaction to the growing awareness in recent years of the "dark side" of US history, which has been systematically excluded from public education from the beginning.

Texas, 2023

By mid-2023, the list of books banned in Texas reached 15,000 titles. Public schools, public libraries, and prisons are subject to the bans. Students and prisoners are free to read material such as Mein Kampf and The Protocols of the Elders of Zion, but all the works of William Shakespeare are forbidden, as are any books that present a view of history other than the standard white nationalist narrative.

Separately, but in the same state in the same year, the largest school district in Houston fired all teachers and librarians. They required teachers to re-apply for their jobs as if they were applying as new hires. Naturally, most of the good teachers left. The librarians were not replaced. Instead, school libraries were converted into disciplinary centers where children are sent for punishment. Those schools no longer have libraries at all. Interestingly, this process affects predominantly Latino and predominantly Black schools.

Oklahoma, 2022

New York City public libraries offer digital library cards to students aged 13 to 21, enabling them to download around 300,000 eBooks. States with strong conservative governments worry this will enable indoctrination of children. In Oklahoma, criminal charges will be brought against any teacher who makes a student aware of this program, even if they do nothing else. The authorities have also threatened to take steps to remove the teacher's professional credentials permanently.

Florida, 2023

The state of Florida passed a law making it a criminal offense - a felony, like armed robbery or murder - for a teacher to make books available to students, except books explicitly approved by the state government. In 2023, eight of the nation's leading textbook publishers were surprised when Florida excluded several mathematics texts from the approved list, including McGraw Hill, Cengage Learning, Macmillan Learning, and Savvas Learning.

A spokesperson for Macmillan Learning said they were "surprised and disappointed that a statistics text...was not on the approved list...with no explanation." Per ABC News report, a spokesperson for Savvas Learning said "Savvas has a long and successful track record of working with the Florida Department of Education to ensure that our instructional materials deliver high-quality, research-based pedagogy designed intentionally to align with the current Florida standards for mathematics." The same report states that several publishers said they are at a loss to understand what to change in a mathematics textbook to comply with Florida's new political requirements.

The approved list for Florida as of the time of reporting, available at https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/5574/urlt/2122MathAdoptedMaterials.pdf when we checked on Jan 31, 2023, offers teachers few choices for mathematics textbooks, and nearly all appear to come from the same source - a politically-vetted source.

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, when announcing the new regulations, said, "It seems that some publishers attempted to slap a coat of paint on an old house built on the foundation of Common Core, and indoctrinating concepts like race essentialism, especially, bizarrely, for elementary school students."

As we know, Common Core does not dictate content, but rather specifies skills or capabilities that students should attain in English and mathematics at particular grade levels. It does not address other subjects that might be politically sensitive, such as history or political science.

We will go further and state that it does not "seem" as if publishers are doing anything like what DeSantis claims they are doing.

A conservative information source sympathetic to Florida's limitation of educational materials, Not the Bee, reported on Jan 30, 2023, as follows:

"Duval County Public Schools has removed all books off the shelves of their library and classrooms to "err on the side of caution" in complying with Florida's anti-indoctrination laws.

"The[y] said the removal was to review the books for content that state authorities might say falls under the new guidelines because they don't want to face prosecution for indoctrinating school children, a third degree felony."

Not the Bee goes on to criticize the school system for overreacting to the law, even suggesting they removed all books as a "stunt" to make DeSantis "look bad." Yet, it is prudent to err on the side of caution when normal activities are suddenly recategorized as felonies, with no clarity about where the authorities might decide to draw the line. After all, they have already banned mathematics and statistics books, which have no political content at all.

If Texas is poised for Nazi-style festive public book-burning events, Florida may well be headed for a Fahrenheit 451 scenario. Of course, if that book is banned then Floridians will not see it coming.
