Technology and homeschooling

Digital technologies offer both benefits and risks for educating young people. Technologies like educational software, artificial intelligence (AI), augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and others can enable homeschoolers to craft effective learning experiences quickly and with less effort than before these things were invented. From the learner's point of view, technology can streamline the study process and help them make optimal use of their time.

An example is YouTube's "Learning Mode" add-on, which can connect the dots between the video the student is watching and supporting information that they otherwise would have to look up separately. Another is ChatGPT, which can be used to generate a personalized study plan and to act as a study "partner."

There are negative considerations in the use of these technologies that must be balanced against the potential benefits.

Depending too heavily on AI to generate educational materials and lesson plans may cause you to overlook details or produce inaccurate information. In many cases, you must spend at least as much time reviewing what the tools produce as you would have done to create the materials yourself. You can't rely on them to generate educational material on subjects with which you, yourself, are unfamiliar.

The situation is not entirely positive from the learner's point of view, either. Studies have shown that overuse of these technologies can physically degrade the user's cognitive function. You must control the amount of time your learners spend using these tools, as well as the intensity or depth with which they use them.
