Non-free Music Learning Resources

Online, downloadable, printable, physical learning resources not free of charge.

General

  • Bass Buzz
    A series of 30 online electric bass lessons for about $200.
  • Coursera
    Coursera is a portal for online training courses in a wide range of subjects. The courses available vary over time as people add new material. In the general area of "music" the courses range from music theory to how-to-play-the-[insert instrument here] to courses on music production, songwriting, and the music business. The offerings are somewhat haphazard, as Coursera is not a dedicated music teaching site. It just happens that some of their course authors teach music.
  • Flowkey
    The site offers a customized learning path for the piano and features an interactive online keyboard, so you don't need to have a piano or other keyboard instrument to practice. It is similar to Simply but less "slick." Warning: The cost is not shown until you reach the point of "joining."
  • Make Music
    This is a resource for music educators and students. It offers various printable items and practice materials for sight-reading and etudes. More interesting is the interactive feature that lets the user choose their part from an orchestral or band piece and play along with a synthesized performance of the piece. Beware of the pricing shown on the website, as it includes a bulk purchase discount that will not apply to a single individual subscription.
  • MuseScore Courses
    MuseScore is an open source music notation program that has not only superseded commercial products in quality and popularity, but has become the nexus of a large community of musicians from beginners to professionals. The MuseScore organization offers free and commercial resources for sharing scores and learning about music. They offer numerous online video courses for instrumentalists, vocalists, composers, and arrangers. There is some free content, but to access everything you need a paid subscription.
  • Simply
    The site offers a customized learning path based on your goals for learning piano or guitar. There is a very well-crafted presentation of videos showing happy people playing music, and the site takes you through a series of 20-25 questions about your musical interests and goals to craft a guided lesson plan for you. Warning: The cost is not shown until you reach the point of "joining."
  • Thumbtack
    Thumbtack is an online service to help you find qualified music teachers near you. In fact, Thumbtack lists all kinds of services other than music lessons. If you're looking for a private teacher for an instrument or voice, or lessons in music theory or composition, you might find someone through this service. Information about available teachers is displayed, including their rates.
  • Yousician
    This site focuses on learning to play an instrument or to sing. When we checked it out, they offered online lessons in guitar, piano, bass, ukulele, and voice with an emphasis on popular music and social playing. Warning: This is clearly not a free offering, yet they do not make the cost apparent on the site. We were unable to find the pricing listed anywhere.
