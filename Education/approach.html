Approach

This page summarizes the general approach to home schooling with this model. It does not provide detailed lesson plans or curricula.

Two key requirements

There are two key requirements for achieving the goal of education through home schooling:

  • The three aspects of personal development listed on the goal of education page; and
  • The specific requirements stipulated in the State guidelines for home schoolers (varies by State).

Even if you believe your educational plan is superior to your State's home schooling guidelines, it is mandatory that you follow all the documented requirements for home schooling, or risk having your program invalidated. On the bright side, it is highly likely that everything required by your State can be mapped to the three aspects of personal development either directly or indirectly. Another benefit of following a State home schooling program is that it will encourage you to develop self-discipline and administrative consistency. These things will help your learners advance as you wish them to advance.

Learning objectives and learning activities

Every short-term learning objective and every learning activity must be designed to address at least one point under the three aspects of personal development or one requirement stipulated in the State home schooling guidelines. There are no "random" activities or ad hoc lesson objectives.

Consideration of the learner's developmental stage

Learning activities and expectations for the learner's ability to demonstrate achievement must be tailored to the learner's present stage of development. Activities and expectations will be different for a 5-year-old than for a 15-year-old. That said, if your State's guidelines require any specific standardized testing at particular ages or at particular milestones in their curriculum, then you must meet those requirements.

Alternative methods

The Coronavirus pandemic of 2020 has provided home schoolers with an opportunity to reconsider and reevaluate traditional and conventional methods of teaching. A return to the status quo ante may not be the best way to move forward with education.

The Internet and computer technology in general offer more ways for learners to acquire information and learn skills, and access to more different teachers and practitioners, than were ever available to them in a traditional classroom setting. Consider taking full advantage of this.

Listening to a lecture or reading a book are not the only ways a person can learn. Look for video games, interactive web sites, and other non-traditional resources that can help in your home schooling program.

Time

Time is the tyrant that looms over everything we do. Many parents, forced into home schooling in 2020 due to the pandemic, have found they lack the time to devote sufficient attention to their children's "remote" attendance in school.

There is an underlying assumption that children must spend a solid six to eight hours per day, five days per week, to absorb all the material they are expected to learn in a public school curriculum. In most States, the academic requirements of public school are not very stringent. Pennsylvania and New York are notable exceptions, but for the most part the traditional classroom setting has proven to be an inefficient way for students to learn. They learn very little compared to the amount of time they have invested in classroom attendance.

By planning learning activities that take different forms from the classroom environment, breaking up learning opportunities into small chunks, opportunistically pointing out learning points as everyday events occur, gamifying the learning process, and spreading activities throughout the day, it is possible to enable learners to pick up more information and skills in less overall time than they can do by sitting still for six hours, looking at paper and listening to a teacher drone. Make learning a natural part of daily life rather than a chore to be scheduled and timed. This will reduce the stress involved in home schooling.

Potential conflicts

It is unlikely the State's curriculum will conflict with anything you wish your learners to know, but in some subject areas the material approved for public schools may be inadequate to meet your own, presumably higher standards of education. There are three subject areas in particular that may fall into this category:

  • History
  • Religion
  • Science

Due to the market dominance of Texas and California in the production of approved textbooks for public schools nationwide, there are some peculiarities in the way these subjects are taught in public schools. In some cases, the content of textbooks differs by State, as well. See this NPR piece for further information: Analysis Finds Big Differences in School Textbooks in States with Differing Politics.

Influence of Texas on textbook content

Texas brings a conservative, Christian perspective to school textbooks. Details vary by State, but in general this manifests as

  • Missing information in history, economics, or political science, when the information might present the United States in a negative light, even when the information is accurate.
  • A lack of any academic teaching of religious belief systems, and a lack of any mention of the importance of religion in history and world cultures, despite the fact religion has had a profound impact on all cultures worldwide throughout history.
  • A softening of principles of critical thinking and the scientific method to accommodate faith-based pseudo-science, such as "creation science."

Influence of California on textbook content

California brings a liberal perspective that attempts to encourage diversity and openness, and often goes beyond the point of diminishing returns. In general, this manifests as

  • The omission of any information in history, sociology, or political science, when the information might potentially "offend" someone or cause feelings of shame or guilt.
  • The tendency to encourage students to accept any and all perspectives and beliefs as equally valid and deserving of our respect, including things that history has demonstrated to be harmful or that many people consider evil, such as slavery, Communism, or Fascism.
  • Inaccurate presentation of the relative weight of minority issues. While everyone is equally deserving of respect, it is simply not the case that 75% of married couples are same-sex, although 75% of classroom materials may feature same-sex couples, or that there is always exactly one Black person in any given group of people, although many classroom materials feature a single, carefully-placed Black figure in any drawing or photo of groups.

Our recommendation in these cases is to cover the material required in your State so that your learners can pass the standardized tests, and supplement that material with additional information that fills in the gaps and provides proper historical and philosophical context for the information. Learners must gain all available knowledge, but must also understand what not to say on a standardized test, to meet the State's expectations.

Home schooling for religious reasons

Many parents choose home schooling specifically to ensure their children receive guidance within a certain religious tradition. Public schools explicitly avoid that, as the US Constitution prohibits the government from establishing an official religion. Public schools usually err on the side of caution by simply not mentioning religion at all, and prohibiting clothing or student activities that might be construed by others as "religious."

This leads to silliness such as Muslims being unable to have a room set aside for prayer, or Christians being prohibited from setting up a Nativity scene during the Christmas season, even when these things are organized by and for students, and have nothing to do with the government imposing a state religion.

If you are home schooling to provide a faith-based education to your children, our recommendation is to supplement the State's curriculum with your own religious lessions or those provided by your church, as well as providing your own faith-based interpretation of material provided in State-approved learning materials.

In addition, to ensure your children grow into fully-functional adult members of a diverse society (as called for on the goal of education page), avoid indoctrinating them in such a way that they disrespect fellow citizens who have different religious beliefs.

There is a point of view in the country these days that the very existence of a person who believes differently from oneself is an intrusion on one's religious freedom. We consider that absurd. The fact someone has beliefs different from your own has no effect on your ability to practice your own religion.
