Sample Lesson Plan for "Orbital Mechanics", Child Level

Item Content
Grade Level(s) 1-6
Learning Objectives Awareness that objects in space orbit around each other, and some of the relevant principles.
Prerequisites None
Duration 45 minutes
00:05. Balloon Activity Teacher distributes balloons to the learners. Teacher demonstrates how to blow up a balloon and hold it without tying it off. Now: Hold your balloons up high! We'll count down from five. When we get to zero, let go of your balloon! Five - four- three - two - one - liftoff!
What happened to the balloons? Why did they zoom away from your hands? (Discussion - at this point there are no wrong answers; stimulate interest and encourage exploration).
Let's find out more about what's going on here.
00:05 Watch Video 1 Watch the video, "Easy Orbital Mechanics I". Discuss new vocabulary words, such as: orbit, gravity, zero-G, trajectory, etc.
Imagine flying a rocket into space. What are some words you would use to describe the experience? (Vocabulary-building and expression exercise - "exciting", "scary", "loud", etc.).
What could you see from your spaceship when you look back at Earth?
Let's find out more!
00:05 Ball Toss Activity Teacher distributes balls to learners.
Toss your ball straight up and catch it when it comes back down.
Did you notice the ball starts going up fast, then slows down, then speeds up again when it comes back down? Try it again and watch how the ball slows down and speeds up.
Why do you think that happens? (Discussion - no wrong answers.)
Do you think spaceships and moons work the same way as balls? Let's see.
00:05 Watch Video 2 Watch the video, "Easy Orbital Mechanics II". Discuss new vocabulary words, such as: velocity, etc.
If your spaceship is in the same orbit as another spaceship, how can you catch up with the other spaceship? (slow down to change the shape of the orbit, then speed up to match the other spaceship)
What happens if we try to fly to the moon by aiming straight at it?
00:05 Guided Discussion What if we are in orbit around Earth and we want to go to Mars? How can we do that?
Let's find out more!
00:05 Watch Video 3 Watch the video, "Easy Orbital Mechanics III". Discuss new vocabulary words, such as: frame of reference, gravity well, fuel consumption, etc.
To go to Mars we have to think about our orbit around Earth, Earth's orbit around the Sun, and Mars' orbit around the Sun, so it's more complicated than going to the Moon.
Why do we have to wait a while before we return to Earth from Mars?
00:15 Arts and Crafts Activity Learners self-organize into groups of 2 or 3 people. Teacher distributes materials for the arts and crafts activity. Learners make a representation of things they have learned, like the Sun, Earth, Moon, Mars, and spaceships in orbit.
Preparation
  • One balloon per learner.
  • Balls that are safe to throw in the room (or go outside).
  • One panel of posterboard per 2 or 3 learners.
  • Construction paper.
  • Safety scissors - one pair per 2 or 3 learners.
  • Crayons or markers.
  • Cut out photos of the sun, planets, moons, satellites, and spaceships.
  • Optionally, get stickers of the sun, planets, etc.
  • Optionally, learners can draw the sun, planets, etc.
  • Age-appropriate paste or glue.
  • Physical or electronic rewards that you want to give the learners for completing the lesson.
  • Cue up videos:
    • Easy Orbital Mechanics I
    • Easy Orbital Mechanics II
    • Easy Orbital Mechanics III
Additional Resources None
