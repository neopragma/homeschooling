Free Learning Resources - Life Skills - Emotional Development - Learned Helplessness

General Information

The feeling of "helplessness" is the belief there is nothing one can do to improve one's circumstances or to overcome difficulties in life. The idea of "learned" helplessness is that negative experiences can cause us to lose confidence in ourselves and "give up" in the face of challenges in life.

There is a mental health aspect to this, as profound or long-term helplessness can lead to depression.

As teachers, mentors, and parents, we want to help our students recognize the signs of learned helplessness in themselves and to overcome its effects so they can become functional, independent adults.

Learned helplessness can result from experiences over which we have no control, such as illness, injury, or trauma due to acts of nature, crime, or war. We can become "beaten down" and begin to believe there is nothing we can do.

Learned helplessness is also induced intentionally by political or relgious leaders or by employers as a way to control people by keeping them passive and submissive.

Regardless of the cause, it is possible for people to recognize and defeat learned helplessness.

From Simply Psychology:

Learned helplessness is a psychological state in which an individual, after repeated exposure to uncontrollable negative events, believes they are powerless to change their situation, even when opportunities to do so arise.

This leads to passivity, decreased motivation, and a sense of hopelessness, which can persist even when circumstances change and control is possible.

Learned helplessness is often associated with depression.
