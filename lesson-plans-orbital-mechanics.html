Sample Lesson Plans for "Orbital Mechanics"

The topic, "Orbital Mechanics," would be part of a series of units pertaining to Newtonian physics, astronomy, and/or spaceflight. At higher grade/age levels, it brings in some algebra and geometry as well as problem-solving and critical thinking.

The page on Pedagogical Methods lists several approaches to teaching. Your lesson plans will have to be designed to support your preferred way of teaching. You probably won't be able to use this example - or any example - exactly as-is. In fact, this example probably has too much "stuff" in it to be practical. The intent is to provide you with several ideas to consider when creating your own lesson plans.

This set of three lesson plans touches on several of the ideas on the Pedagogical Methods page, but not in great depth:

  • Experiential Learning
  • Flipped Learning
  • Modified Socratic Method
  • Explaining a Topic at Multiple Levels of Difficulty

They also take into account some of the ideas from the Developmental Stages page, especially the categories of Cognitive and Language development.

The plans try to connect parts of the lessons with specific elements of the Core Standards, to reinforce the development of comprehension and reasoning skills.

Finally, the lesson plans try to provide something for learners who rely on text, video and audio, or kinesthetic learning, as well as using games as a vehicle for learning.

Bear in mind this is only meant as an example. You don't need to create lesson plans that use multiple teaching methods and all that. Just draw ideas from the examples that you can use, and adapt them to your own needs and preferences.

Orbital Mechanics - Child

Learners watch very short videos (under 3 minutes) to illustrate basic concepts. No math required. Then they do an arts-and-crafts activity to make a physical representation of the sun, planets, moons, satellites, and/or spacecraft on posterboard using cut-out photos, construction paper, scissors, paste or glue, and crayons or markers. There is no formal assessment, but there is a discussion at the end in which the children are encouraged to let their imaginations soar.

Orbital Mechanics - Adolescent

Learners watch the 2008 NASA video, "Orbits and Kepler's Laws." Then there is a verbal assessment to reinforce terminology and concepts. This is followed by a written assessment and debrief. Finally, the learners play the video game, "Kerbal Space Program," which you (the teacher) have already played up to a point that introduces the concepts of orbital mechanics.

Lesson Plan

Orbital Mechanics - Teen

Learners watch the 1989 NASA video, "Space Flight: The Application of Orbital Mechanics." It provides a clear, step-by-step explanation of the basic concepts at a good pace, without unnecessary complication. There is a verbal assessment to reinforce terminology and concepts, with expectations set higher than at the Adolescent level. The learners play the video game, "Kerbal Space Program," which you (the teacher) have already played up to a point that introduces the concepts of orbital mechanics. The difference between the Teen and Adolescent levels is the expectations we have of the learners. This is followed by a more-detailed written document compiled by Robert Braeunig between 1997 and 2013 that has more mathematics than the Adolescent material. The document provides several problems the learners can work through to strengthen their knowledge of orbital mechanics. The lesson concludes with a group debrief.
